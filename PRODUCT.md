# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Engineering leaders and CTOs evaluating senior engineers for high-impact roles. They scan fast, read between the lines, and assess signal-to-noise ratio instinctively. Their context: evaluating whether Lenny thinks at the level the role demands and whether the craft on display justifies a conversation. The job to be done: leave with enough confidence to reach out.

## Product Purpose

A developer portfolio for Lenny Peters — Senior Software Engineer II specializing in AI-to-production systems, React, TypeScript, and Next.js. The portfolio exists to convert qualified engineering leaders into conversations: not by listing skills, but by demonstrating them through the medium itself. Success = a CTO or engineering leader reaching out because the portfolio itself convinced them.

## Brand Personality

Sharp, opinionated, forward-thinking.

The texture: an engineer who lives in the terminal, has strong takes, and ships things that work. Not a polished consultant — a builder who has opinions. The cyberpunk/hacker aesthetic is authentic to how Lenny builds, not a costume.

## Anti-references

- **Corporate LinkedIn professionalism** — buttoned-up, safe, designed to offend no one and impress no one. The portfolio must have a specific POV, not a resume layout.
- **Generic skill-list portfolios** — big headshot, skills grid, download my CV. No personality, no evidence of craft.
- **Over-engineered agency showpieces** — scroll-jacking, heavy motion, more demo than substance. Motion earns its place or it does not exist.

## Design Principles

1. **The medium is the message.** The portfolio itself signals engineering quality. Code polish, interaction precision, and visual craft are the proof of skill — not a description of it.
2. **Opinionated over safe.** Every design choice should feel specific to Lenny, not templated. A strong aesthetic POV is the differentiation. Avoiding the LinkedIn lane means committing to a voice.
3. **Terminal authenticity.** The cyberpunk/hacker register is where Lenny works, not a costume. Use it with precision: scanlines and neon glow should feel like the real environment, not decoration.
4. **Signal over noise.** Engineering leaders scan fast. Every element earns its place. Information density over filler. Content that cannot survive a 10-second skim should not be there.
5. **Show, do not list.** Demonstrate capability through craft, not enumeration. Responsiveness, performance, and attention to detail are a live code review.

## Accessibility and Inclusion

WCAG AA throughout: contrast ratios >= 4.5:1 for body text, >= 3:1 for large text, keyboard navigation, semantic HTML, screen reader support. Respect prefers-reduced-motion — the terminal aesthetic must degrade gracefully, not break. Color choices should be color-blind-safe where possible (the cyan/green palette has sufficient luminance contrast for deuteranopia, but avoid using color as the sole signal carrier).
